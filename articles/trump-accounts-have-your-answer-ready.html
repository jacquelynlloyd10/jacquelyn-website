Trump Accounts: Have Your Answer Ready

On September 29, Treasury issued temporary regulations to automatically open a Trump Account for eligible children who don't already have one, starting around October 1. Treasury estimates more than 60 million new accounts this year.

What this means for you

You don't have to do anything. But many of your employees' children now have an account, and some employees will ask whether the company plans to contribute. Let's get your answer ready.

What hasn't changed

  • Employers may contribute up to $2,500 per year, tax-free, and that counts toward the account's $5,000 annual limit. The rules for how employer contributions work are still proposed, not final.
  • An automatically opened account can't receive employer or parent money until a parent claims and activates it.
  • The $1,000 federal deposit isn't automatic. A parent has to elect it.

Pick your posture now

  1. Not contributing, but ready. “We're not contributing at this time. Here's where you can learn more about the account.”
  2. Educating. Share neutral resources so employees can claim and activate accounts on their own.
  3. Contributing. Talk to your benefits broker or counsel first. Don't promise payroll or pre-tax options until they confirm what's actually possible.

This quarter

Decide your posture, give managers a one- or two-sentence answer, and add Trump Accounts to your broker's year-end agenda.

Source: Federal Register, Trump Accounts temporary regulations (T.D. 10056), published September 30, 2026: federalregister.gov
